LING 2027 revision

Four topics, each with a re-teach page and a practice page, plus a worked mock built on the course's own midterm-preparation problem set. The numbering is the course's own: Topic 1 phonetics, Topic 2 abstraction/contrast/predictability, Topic 3 phonotactics, Topic 4 alternations. Midterm: Thursday 29 October, week 7.

How to use this pack

  1. Read the re-teach page first, then do its practice page the same day. Each re-teach page carries the source page numbers, so anything you doubt can be checked against the PDF in the course folder in a minute.
  2. Do the practice page cold. Write your answer before opening the step reveals — the steps model the order an examiner wants (describe, then name, then motivate), which carries more marks than the final label.
  3. Keep the course's notation. Phonemes in /slashes/, surface forms in [brackets], features as [+high], environments as ___X. Descriptions in the course's fixed order: consonants "(other) – voicing – place – manner", vowels "height – backness – rounding".
  4. Finish on the mock. Practice 05 works the three Tutorial E problems — the set the lecturer issued for this week's revision tutorial — with model answers in the course's answer template.
Exam shape — published

The Topic 4 deck states the midterm's scope on its last slide (PDF p. 31): the paper "covers everything we have done so far, including: 1. Phonetics (including non-English, but you will be given an IPA chart with the exam) 2. Abstraction, contrast, predictability 3. Phonotactics 4. Alternations", and advises checking your knowledge "with p-sets".

Two consequences worth acting on. The IPA chart is supplied with the paper, so the skill tested is reading the chart quickly, not recalling it. And the phonetics questions may use data from languages other than English. No mark weights and no paper structure are published anywhere in the course materials, so none are quoted in this pack.

Topics

TOPIC 01

Phonetics: the vocal tract and articulation

The three systems, the parameters of speech, manner and place, vowels, and how to turn a sound into an articulatory description and a transcription. Weeks 1–3; Zsiga Ch. 1–4 and the Topic 1 deck.

Re-teachPractice
 TOPIC 02

Abstraction, contrast, predictability

Why the phoneme is an abstraction, minimal pairs, complementary distribution, free variation, and the phonemic-analysis procedure with the complications that make it hard. Week 4; Zsiga Ch. 10 and the Topic 2 deck.

Re-teachPractice
 TOPIC 03

Phonotactics

Actual, possible and impossible words, absolute versus statistical generalisations, borrowings as evidence, and how to state a constraint so that it can be tested. Week 5; Zsiga §11.1, the Topic 3 deck and Tutorial D.

Re-teachPractice
 TOPIC 04

Alternations

Allomorphs and the environments that select them, the course's HOW/WHAT/WHY analysis, the twelve rows of Table 11.1, and the two ways of formalising a rule. Week 6; Zsiga Ch. 11.2–3 with the Topic 4 deck and Tutorial D.

Practice pages

PRACTICE 01

Articulation and transcription drills

Descriptions in the course's fixed order, symbol to description to word and back, vowel labels, a non-English inventory decoded from the chart, and the cases where one symbol does not equal one sound.

 PRACTICE 02

Phonemic analysis drills

Minimal versus near-minimal pairs, the three distributions, natural classes, the four-step procedure on constructed data, and one dataset that is deliberately underdetermined so you practise saying what the data permits.

 PRACTICE 03

Phonotactic drills

Constraints from a lexicon plus speaker judgements, actual/possible/impossible sorting, absolute versus statistical with a trap, and loanword repairs as the test of whether a stated constraint is real.

 PRACTICE 04

Alternation drills

Environment lists, UR choice by the elsewhere case, type naming, the Quiz 4 term definitions, and the answer template worked on invented data.

 PRACTICE 05

Mock midterm

The three Tutorial E problems — Hungarian dative, Spanish casual speech, acquisition errors — worked in full, with the traps named and the analyses the data also permits flagged.
